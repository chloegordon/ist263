Travel Tips & Lessons






Paris

One of the most gorgeous cities. My biggest tip is to buy tickets for the Eiffel Tower in advance. When I was here, we had to wait in long lines, and once we finally reached the front, we found out the elevators broke (another tip: walk it).

Moreover, wear comfortable shoes, as you will be doing a ton of walking. If you want to use a tour guide, book only half-day tours. I highly recommend having a tour guide in the morning up until lunch, and then having the rest of your days free.

The food in Paris is incredible, but be sure to book a reservation ahead of time; restaurants usually open their reservations 3 months in advance.

Cooking classes, sightseeing, eating, and seeing the culture are all things that make Paris so amazing, and I highly recommend visiting. Follow these tips, and I will ensure you will have a fantastic, smooth time.


South Africa

The only words to describe this trip: unbelievable, incredible, beautiful, and cultural. If possible, I recommend that everyone visit South Africa. I was away for about 12 days and split my time between a safari and Cape Town, which is a beautiful city.

When planning your trip, I highly recommend setting aside 7 days for safaris. Yes, 7 days. You need 7 days to see everything, especially the Big Five. We split our time between two different hotels within the same national park, which allowed us to see other animals since the parks are hundreds of miles apart. A safari is something we never got sick of doing, and it was truly an incredible experience.

The second half of our trip took us to Cape Town, where we hiked Tabletop Mountain, visited the closest point in Africa to Antarctica, spotted penguins, and explored the area on foot. At Tabletop Mountain, you have the option to take cable cars up, but I highly recommend hiking instead, as it makes the experience 10x more rewarding. The penguins are a MUST-SEE, too. We easily covered Cape Town in 4 days, and while you can always extend your trip, I don't think it is necessary.







Japan

I highly recommend researching Japanese food before you go. Let me warn you, the sushi there is nothing like it is back in the United States, which I am sure does not come as a shock to many people. 7/11 is a must-go to when you get to Japan, and yes, I promise, the egg salad sandwich truly is as good as it looks.

Some of my favorite restaurants in the area include Lurra, Kyūbey, Chao Chao Gyoza Shanjo Kiyamachi, and Ginza Kagari Main Branch. Each restaurant offers something unique. At Chao Chao Gyoza Shanjo Kyamachi in Kyoto, I strongly suggest arriving 10 minutes before it opens; the queue can be overwhelming. The fried chicken there is exceptionally good, and during my visit, cash was the only accepted payment. At Ginza Kagari Main Branch, I highly recommend the cold dipping noodles. They do not accept reservations, and the line is long, but it moves efficiently.

Moreover, there are two well-known markets there: the Tsukiji fish market in Tokyo and Nishiki Market in Kyoto. The Tsukiji fish market is a must-visit, and if you go there, Nishiki Market is something you can skip.

Now to discuss the sightseeing. You'll visit many shrines, but the most gorgeous one we went to that is not worth skipping is the Golden Pavilion Temple in Kyoto. We did take a trip down to Hiroshima, where seeing where the atomic bomb was dropped was very powerful, but taking the ferry to see the Itsukushima Shrine is something that individuals can skip.

When I went, we spent 5 nights in Tokyo, 1 night in Hakone, and 4 nights in Kyoto. We went to Hakone to see Mt. Fuji, but unfortunately, it was too cloudy to see it, and that's usually the case. We were able to see it from afar on the bullet train we took, but we didn't expect to see it clearly.

Make sure to save time for shopping. Vintage shopping in Japan is unbelievable, as is their skincare and many other things. Finally, I highly recommend that if you want to be a tour guide, try to have a consistent one for each location and only offer half days. Japan is truly an incredible place, and I want you all to learn from my mistakes.












Out West Trip (Utah, Arizona, & Vegas)

One of my favorite trips I have ever been on was my visit to the West Coast. We went to three different places: Utah, Arizona, and Las Vegas.

In Utah and Arizona, we visited numerous national parks, including the Grand Canyon, Bryce Canyon, Zion National Park, Antelope Canyon (upper and lower), and Horseshoe Bend. My top picks among these places are Bryce Canyon, which I highly recommend visiting and hiking, and Zion National Park, where we had the opportunity to rappel down mountains. Additionally, the lower Antelope Canyon is worth exploring. Horseback riding is also an option, but it can be a bit intimidating.

The second half of my trip was very different, and I enjoyed it. It was more relaxing in Las Vegas, so I definitely recommend doing that second. Here, we enjoyed magic shows, Coca-Cola taste testing, amusement rides, and so much more. When I went on this trip, I was just a child, so going to the casino was not an option for me. That said, Las Vegas is still very kid-friendly, and if you are considering traveling with kids, I definitely recommend it.
